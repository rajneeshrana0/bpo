Dictation Practice — 70 Sentences

In dictation you're graded on writing exactly what's spoken — spelling, punctuation, capitalisation, and small grammar words (a/an/the, 's, contractions) all count. Hit Listen to hear the sentence read aloud (US accent) without seeing the text, write down what you heard, then hit Record to speak the sentence back yourself and play it back to check your pronunciation. Reveal only once you're done. Sentences flagged watch have a common trap: a contraction, a tag question, or a perfect tense that's easy to mishear or misspell.

Email Writing

Every topic in this set follows the same shape. Learn the shape once, and you can answer any of them under time pressure.

The universal 4-part structure

  1. Greeting — "Dear / Hello [Name],"
  2. Purpose line — one sentence stating why you're writing (context + what you want).
  3. Three supporting points — one short paragraph per required theme. Name the theme, then give one concrete reason or example. Always use exactly the three themes given in the prompt — don't add a fourth or skip one.
  4. Closing — a polite offer to discuss further + sign-off ("Thanks & Regards,").

Practice topics

Two full worked examples

Recognising a co-worker (Anna → Mr. Henderson)

Hello Mr. Henderson,
I hope this email finds you well. As you know, our company has a program to reward outstanding workers — I'd like to nominate Anna, whose work on the recent project has been excellent.purpose line
First, she has strong work habits and sets an example for the rest of the team.theme 1 — work habits
Second, she consistently brings new and well-thought-out ideas that have helped us win clients.theme 2 — new ideas
Third, her customer satisfaction scores are consistently high, since she is proficient and polite with every client.theme 3 — customer satisfaction
Please let me know if you'd like to discuss this further.
Thanks & Regards,closing

Requesting a team party (→ Mr. Nelson)

Dear Mr. Nelson,
My team and I are very pleased about completing the recent project, and I wanted to suggest that we celebrate the effort with a team party.purpose line
We put in significant overtime over the past month to meet the deadline.theme 1 — hours worked
The project itself was a real success and reflected well on the whole department.theme 2 — success of the project
A small celebration would also be great for morale going into the next project.theme 3 — motivation
Thanks & Regards,closing

Passage Reconstruction

This section tests paraphrasing — saying the same thing in different words without losing any fact. Five moves cover almost every case:

The 5 paraphrase moves

  1. Swap synonyms — "confusion" → "misunderstanding", "unfortunately" → "because of"
  2. Reorder clauses — move the reason clause before or after the main clause
  3. Change voice — active ↔ passive ("he refused" → "it was refused")
  4. Combine or split sentences — two short sentences into one, or vice versa
  5. Keep every fact — names, dates, numbers, and the core sequence of events must all survive

HR Round (50)

Standard HR interview questions with model answers. Use these as a starting structure, then swap in your own real details where marked — a memorized-sounding answer is easy for an interviewer to spot.

One question worth preparing for specifically

"You're a co-founder running your own company — why are you looking for a BPO role?" A direct, honest answer works best: you're building a startup long-term, and you want a stable, structured income and professional communication experience in the near term. Confidence here reads better than avoiding the topic.
